About EV Saves

Real EV running costs. No spin. No agenda.

Our Mission

EV Saves exists because the information available to people considering an electric vehicle is almost always wrong — or at least incomplete. Manufacturer marketing focuses on the best-case savings. Sceptical media focuses on worst-case charging costs. Neither tells you what switching to an EV will actually cost you, week to week, in your country, at your electricity rate, with your driving habits.

Our mission is simple: give drivers in the UK, Australia and the United States honest, data-driven tools to calculate their real EV running costs before they commit to one of the biggest purchases of their lives. No fluff. No agenda. Just the numbers.

What We Do

We build free online calculators tailored to each region. Every calculator uses locally relevant prices and real-world efficiency figures, and shows you the full calculation so you can check our working.

  • UK calculators — 19 tools covering EV vs petrol, home charging, TCO, Tesla and BYD models, salary sacrifice, BIK tax, fleet costs, road tax savings and more.
  • Australia calculators — 15 tools covering EV vs petrol, stamp duty exemptions by state, home charging, solar self-sufficiency, fleet costs, used EV values and more.
  • US calculators — 14 tools covering EV vs gas, the $7,500 federal tax credit, home charging, model-specific costs (Tesla Model 3, Hyundai Ioniq 6 and more), TCO and fleet calculators.

We also cover car modification calculators — horsepower gains, quarter-mile estimates, turbo sizing and boost pressure — because many EV drivers are enthusiasts who want the same rigour applied to performance numbers.

Our Data Sources

We do not make up prices. Every default value in our calculators is drawn from a named, publicly accessible source and is updated regularly. Our primary sources include:

  • UK electricity prices: Ofgem quarterly price cap figures and Octopus Energy published tariff data.
  • UK petrol/diesel prices: RAC Fuel Watch, which aggregates pump prices from thousands of UK forecourts.
  • UK road tax and BIK: HMRC and DVLA official rate tables.
  • Australian fuel prices: NRMA Fuel Check and state government fuel price monitoring schemes.
  • Australian electricity prices: AEMC Retail Energy Review and state distributor published rates.
  • Australian stamp duty: State Revenue Office publications for each state and territory.
  • US gas prices: US Energy Information Administration (EIA) weekly retail prices.
  • US electricity prices: EIA Electric Power Monthly, broken down by state where possible.
  • EV efficiency figures: WLTP (Europe/UK/Australia) and EPA (US) official test data, cross-referenced against real-world data from EV Database and Spritmonitor.
  • Vehicle prices: Manufacturer UK, AU and US configurators, updated at time of publication.

Each calculator page displays a "prices last updated" date. If you notice a figure that looks out of date, please contact us and we will review it promptly.

Accuracy and Limitations

Our calculators are as accurate as we can make them, but they are estimates — not guarantees. Real-world EV running costs depend on many factors we cannot account for precisely: your individual driving style, local climate (which affects battery efficiency), the specific electricity tariff you are on, and the mix of home versus public charging you use.

We encourage you to enter your own prices rather than rely on the defaults, and to treat the output as a well-informed estimate rather than a definitive figure. The calculators are designed to give you a realistic ballpark and help you ask the right questions — not to replace professional financial advice.

We also note that EV technology, incentive schemes and energy prices change rapidly. A figure that was accurate in early 2026 may be materially different by mid-2026. We do our best to keep up, but cannot guarantee every page is up to the minute.

No Agenda

We are not funded by any EV manufacturer, energy company or government body. EV Saves is supported by display advertising (Google AdSense) and occasional affiliate links to products and services we believe are useful. Affiliate relationships are always disclosed. Our editorial decisions — including which calculators to build and what numbers to use — are made independently.

We are broadly positive about EVs because the evidence broadly supports them as a cheaper-to-run option for most drivers. But we are not cheerleaders. If the numbers show an EV is not cost-effective for a particular user's situation, our calculators will show that too.
