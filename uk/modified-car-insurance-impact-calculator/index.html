The Hidden Cost of Car Modifications: Your Insurance Premium

Car modifications are exciting — but they come with a legal obligation and a financial cost that many drivers underestimate. In the UK, you are legally required to declare any modifications to your insurer when taking out or renewing a policy. Failure to declare modifications can result in your policy being voided, meaning no payout in the event of a claim, and potentially a charge of driving without valid insurance.

Engine modifications have the biggest impact on premiums, typically increasing them by 20–50% depending on the modification. A Stage 1 ECU remap (common on turbocharged diesels and petrols, adding 15–30% more power) is usually the lowest-risk engine mod from an insurance perspective — though it still needs declaring. Forced induction upgrades, camshaft changes, and throttle body modifications attract much larger increases and require specialist modified car insurers.

Suspension modifications — lowering springs, coilovers, strut braces — typically increase premiums by 10–20%. Insurers view altered suspension geometry as a greater liability risk, even if the modification improves handling.

Visual modifications — aftermarket alloys, body kits, spoilers, tinted windows, vinyls — generally have a lower impact on premiums (5–15%) but can affect both theft risk and repair costs. Expensive aftermarket wheels, for instance, can be costly to replace and may not be fully covered under standard policies.

Specialist modified car insurers such as Adrian Flux, Footman James, Performance Direct, and A-Plan can often offer better rates than mainstream insurers for modified vehicles. Always get multiple quotes from both standard and specialist insurers. Some also offer agreed value cover, which is important if you have made significant investments in your build.
