Why UK Businesses Are Switching to EV Fleets in 2026

The shift to electric vehicles across UK business fleets is accelerating rapidly. In 2026, EVs represent the fastest-growing segment in the company car market, driven not just by environmental policy but by compelling financial savings across fuel, taxation, and maintenance. For businesses of all sizes — from sole traders with one company car to enterprises running hundreds of vehicles — the financial case for electrification has rarely been stronger.

Fuel cost savings are the most immediately visible benefit. A fleet car covering 20,000 business miles per year in a 35 MPG petrol car spends approximately £3,070 on fuel at 134p per litre. The equivalent EV, charged primarily at workplace or home charging points at 24.5p/kWh, costs around £1,225 in electricity for the same mileage — a saving of approximately £1,845 per vehicle per year. For businesses with access to smart tariffs at 7p/kWh, the saving increases further to £2,500+ per vehicle annually. Multiply this across a 10 or 20 vehicle fleet and the annual fuel saving runs into tens of thousands of pounds.

Benefit in Kind (BIK) tax is one of the most powerful incentives for EV company car adoption in the UK. The BIK rate for fully electric company cars is just 3% of the vehicle's P11D value in the 2025/26 tax year, rising gradually to 5% in 2027/28. Compare this to petrol cars, which attract BIK rates of 20–37% depending on CO2 emissions. For an employee driving a £35,000 EV and paying income tax at the basic 20% rate, the annual BIK tax bill is approximately £210 (3% × £35,000 × 20%). The same petrol car at a 25% BIK rate would generate a tax bill of £1,750. The employer also pays Class 1A National Insurance Contributions on the BIK value — so both employer and employee benefit significantly from the low EV BIK rate.

HMRC Advisory Electric Rate (AER) for business mileage reimbursement is set at 9p per mile in 2026. This is the rate at which employers can reimburse employees tax-free when they use their own electric vehicle for business travel. This rate is periodically reviewed by HMRC. For employees in company EVs, mileage advisory rates differ under the company car regime.

OZEV infrastructure grants help businesses offset the cost of installing workplace EV charge points. The Workplace Charging Scheme (WCS) provides up to £350 per socket, capped at 40 sockets per business, covering up to 75% of the total installation cost per socket. For a business installing 10 charge points, this can represent a £3,500 grant toward installation costs. Grants must be applied for in advance and require the use of OZEV-approved contractors. Combined with the 100% first-year allowance for EV charging infrastructure under capital allowances rules, the after-tax cost of workplace charging installation is significantly reduced.

Reduced servicing and maintenance costs benefit fleet operators substantially. Electric vehicles have fewer moving parts than ICE vehicles, no oil changes, and dramatically longer brake life due to regenerative braking. Fleet servicing costs for EVs are typically 40–60% lower per vehicle over a 3-year fleet cycle than equivalent petrol models, freeing up significant budget that can be redirected into fleet expansion or other business priorities.
