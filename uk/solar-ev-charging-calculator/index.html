How Solar Panels Can Dramatically Reduce Your EV Charging Costs

Combining solar panels with an electric vehicle is one of the most powerful ways to cut household energy costs in the UK. With a well-sized solar system, a smart charger, and the right electricity tariff, some UK homeowners are achieving EV charging costs that are effectively zero — or close to it. Here is how it works and what you can realistically expect.

Solar generation in the UK is often underestimated. A typical 4 kW solar panel system on a south-facing roof in the UK generates approximately 3,400–3,800 kWh per year. That is enough electricity to power 10,000–15,000 miles of EV driving, depending on your car's efficiency. Systems in the south of England, Wales, and coastal areas generate towards the upper end of this range. A larger 6 kW system — increasingly affordable as panel costs continue to fall — can generate 5,000–5,700 kWh annually, more than enough to cover both household needs and full EV charging for a typical family.

The Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) pays you for the surplus electricity your solar panels export to the grid. In 2026, SEG rates vary by supplier from around 5p/kWh on fixed-rate deals up to variable rates of 15–25p/kWh through services like Octopus Agile during high-demand periods. However, the most financially sensible strategy is usually to consume your solar electricity directly — either in the home or in your EV — rather than exporting it, since the avoided import cost of 24.5p/kWh is typically greater than the SEG export payment you would receive.

Smart EV chargers make solar self-consumption straightforward. Chargers like the myenergi Zappi and Ohme can automatically detect when your solar panels are generating surplus electricity and divert it directly to your car, rather than letting it export to the grid at lower SEG rates. On a sunny summer day with a 4 kW system, a Zappi-style charger can add 20–30 miles of free range to your EV while you are at work or at home. Over a year, this can save £200–£400 in electricity that would otherwise have been exported at a lower rate.

Combining solar with an overnight EV tariff is the optimal strategy for most UK homeowners in 2026. You charge your EV overnight at rates as low as 7p/kWh using a tariff like Octopus Go, then during the day your solar panels supply the household and charge any remaining battery capacity needed. Any surplus solar is exported via SEG. Some homeowners add a battery storage system to capture daytime solar for evening use, though the additional capital cost of a home battery (typically £5,000–£8,000) significantly extends the overall payback period and requires careful modelling of your specific usage pattern.
