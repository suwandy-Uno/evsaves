Is EV Insurance Really More Expensive — And by How Much?

Yes, electric vehicle insurance in the UK is currently more expensive than equivalent petrol car cover. According to ABI data and comparison site surveys, EV drivers pay on average 10–25% more for comprehensive cover than drivers of comparable petrol or diesel vehicles. On an average policy, that translates to an extra £100–£250 per year.

Why does EV insurance cost more? Several factors drive the premium:

Repair costs are the primary driver. EV components — particularly the battery, inverter, and electric motor — are expensive to replace or repair. Specialist equipment is required, and there are far fewer approved EV repair centres in the UK compared to conventional garages. A single battery replacement can cost £5,000–£20,000 depending on the vehicle.

Limited repair network. Even minor accidents can require specialist attention. A cracked undertray that would cost £200 to fix on a petrol car can cost £800+ on an EV if it means inspecting or removing the battery pack. Insurers price this into premiums.

Parts availability. Some EV-specific components, particularly for newer Chinese brands, can have long lead times. Prolonged repair periods mean higher courtesy car costs, which insurers absorb.

Good news: the gap is closing. As more EVs enter the fleet, insurers are gaining more claims data and building better actuarial models. More approved EV repairers are opening. Specialist EV insurers such as Dayinsure, Adrian Flux, and EV-specialist policies from Direct Line and Aviva are becoming more competitive. Shopping around is particularly important for EV insurance — price variation between insurers is larger than for conventional cars. Use comparison sites and get at least three quotes before renewing.

Frequently Asked Questions

EV insurance costs more primarily because repair costs are higher. EV-specific components are expensive, fewer approved repairers exist, and even minor damage can require battery inspection. As the repair network grows and insurers gain more data, the gap is expected to narrow.
Several insurers offer specialist EV policies: Adrian Flux, Dayinsure, and EV-specific products from Aviva, Direct Line, and LV=. Tesla owners can use Tesla Insurance in some cases. Always compare via a comparison site first, then check directly with specialists for additional discounts.
Standard comprehensive EV insurance covers battery damage resulting from accidents. It does not cover gradual degradation (capacity loss over time), which is a manufacturer warranty matter. Some specialist policies offer additional battery protection for power surge or charging cable damage.
Compare at least three quotes from different insurers. Consider a telematics (black box) policy if you drive fewer than 8,000 miles per year. Increase your voluntary excess. Park in a garage or on a driveway overnight. Avoid modifications that aren't declared. Join EV owners clubs that have negotiated group insurance rates.
