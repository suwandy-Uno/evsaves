What Is Your Used EV Actually Worth?

The used EV market in the UK has matured significantly since the first mainstream electric cars arrived in 2011. Values have stabilised after a period of rapid early depreciation, and battery health is now the single most important factor in determining what a second-hand electric car is worth — more so than age or mileage alone.

Depreciation patterns vary significantly by model. Tesla vehicles have historically retained value better than most — a three-year-old Model 3 typically retains 55–65% of its new price, compared to 40–50% for many rivals. Early Nissan Leafs depreciated sharply due to the 24kWh battery's relatively limited range and degradation issues. Newer EVs with larger batteries and better thermal management are holding value far more strongly.

Battery health is the used EV buyer's number-one concern and rightly so. A battery at 85% state of health provides 85% of its original range — a meaningful reduction. Most batteries reach 80% capacity after 100,000–150,000 miles or 8–10 years under normal use. The 80% threshold matters because it is the standard warranty threshold; many manufacturers guarantee 70–80% capacity for 8 years or 100,000 miles. You can check battery health on most EVs using an OBD2 dongle and apps like LeafSpy (Nissan Leaf), Scan My Tesla, or generic tools like SoulEV Spy.

Mileage matters less for EVs than for petrol cars. An EV with 60,000 miles but 90% battery health is worth more than one with 30,000 miles and 75% health. Regenerative braking also means brakes last much longer, reducing the maintenance penalty of higher-mileage used EVs.

Use sites like AutoTrader, heycar, Motorway, and Cazoo to cross-reference real market values in your region. CAP HPI publishes trade valuations used by dealers. Our calculator gives a ballpark estimate based on depreciation curves — always verify against live listings for the specific model you are valuing.
