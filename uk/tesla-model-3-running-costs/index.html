How Much Does a Tesla Model 3 Really Cost to Run in the UK?

The Tesla Model 3 remains one of the most compelling electric cars available in the UK, combining premium technology with genuinely competitive running costs. But headlines about EV savings can mask the full picture. This page breaks down every major cost category so you know exactly what to expect before — and after — you take the plunge.

Tesla Model 3 Specifications That Affect Running Costs

The 2026 Tesla Model 3 (Highland refresh) comes in three trim levels for the UK market: Standard Range RWD, Long Range AWD, and Performance AWD. The most popular is the Long Range, which features a 75 kWh usable battery (nominally 82 kWh gross). In real-world UK driving — accounting for motorway speeds, cold winters, and heating — you can expect around 4.1 miles per kWh on the Standard Range trim. That translates to a genuine real-world range of roughly 240–260 miles per charge, comfortably ahead of most rivals in its class.

Electricity Costs: Home Charging vs Supercharger

Charging a Model 3 from near-empty to 80% at home costs approximately £4.30 on a standard Ofgem-capped tariff (24.5p/kWh as of mid-2026). Switch to an EV-optimised overnight tariff such as Octopus Go — which offers rates as low as 7p/kWh between midnight and 5am — and the same charge drops to around £1.40. Over a typical 10,000 miles per year at 4.1 mi/kWh, you would consume roughly 2,439 kWh annually. At Octopus Go rates that works out to approximately £171 per year; at the standard tariff, around £598. The difference is enormous, and setting up overnight scheduled charging via the Tesla app is straightforward.

Tesla's Supercharger network remains the gold standard for rapid public charging in the UK, with over 1,300 stalls at 160+ locations. Since 2017, new Tesla vehicles no longer include free Supercharging. Current UK Supercharger rates vary by location but average around 75–85p/kWh for non-Tesla account holders and slightly less on a subscription plan. A typical Supercharger top-up from 20% to 80% costs roughly £16–£20 at public rates. For most owners, Superchargers are reserved for longer journeys rather than daily use.

Servicing Costs

This is where Tesla genuinely impresses. There is no oil to change, no conventional alternator, no timing belt, and significantly fewer brake wear items thanks to regenerative braking. Tesla recommends a basic annual check covering brake fluid (every two years), air filter, and wiper blades. Third-party independent EV specialists typically charge £80–£150 for a comprehensive annual service. Adding tyre replacement — Model 3s are heavy and wear tyres relatively quickly — brings annual maintenance costs to around £250–£350 in a typical year. Budget around £120–£200 per year for scheduled servicing as a reasonable baseline.

Insurance

UK car insurance for the Tesla Model 3 sits in insurance group 41–43 depending on trim. This places it in the upper-middle bracket, reflecting the car's performance capabilities and the cost of OEM parts and repairs. Expect annual premiums of approximately £900–£1,300 for a typical 30–45 year old driver with a clean licence, varying significantly by postcode. Specialist EV insurers are beginning to offer more competitive rates as claim data improves. Comparing quotes via aggregators remains the quickest way to identify savings.

Road Tax (Vehicle Excise Duty)

From April 2025, new EVs registered after 1 April 2025 are no longer exempt from road tax. A first-year rate of £10 applies for zero-emission vehicles, followed by the standard rate of £195 per year. Cars with a list price above £40,000 (which includes most new Model 3 variants) also attract the luxury car supplement — an additional £620 per year for the first five years of ownership. That brings road tax for a new Model 3 to approximately £815 per year during that period. Used Model 3s registered before April 2025 may still benefit from zero road tax for existing years of registration.

Company Car BIK Tax

If you drive a Model 3 as a company car, the Benefit in Kind (BIK) rate for zero-emission vehicles is just 3% for 2026/27, rising to 4% in 2027/28 and 5% in 2028/29. On a P11D value of £45,000, a 40% taxpayer would pay just £540 per year in company car tax — versus roughly £4,700 for a comparable petrol executive car. This makes the Model 3 one of the most tax-efficient company cars in the UK market, and many businesses now favour it heavily for this reason.

Depreciation

Tesla has historically been aggressive with price cuts, which has affected residual values. CAP HPI data suggests the Model 3 retains around 45–55% of its value over three years and 36,000 miles — broadly in line with premium German rivals. The Highland refresh has improved residuals somewhat, but depreciation remains the single largest ownership cost for most drivers at £3,000–£5,000 per year depending on variant and usage.

Total Annual Running Cost Summary

For a driver covering 10,000 miles per year using home charging on an EV tariff: electricity approximately £170, servicing £150, insurance £1,100, road tax £195 (or £815 with the luxury supplement), and tyres approximately £200. Excluding depreciation and finance, annual running costs sit at roughly £1,800–£2,400 — compared to £3,500–£4,500 for an equivalent petrol executive car.
