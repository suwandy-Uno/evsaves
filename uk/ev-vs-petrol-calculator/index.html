How Much Can You Really Save Switching from Petrol to Electric?

The decision to switch from a petrol car to an electric vehicle is no longer just about the environment — for most UK drivers, it now makes clear financial sense. But the savings vary enormously depending on how far you drive, where you charge, and what kind of petrol car you are replacing. This calculator cuts through the noise and gives you a personalised, real-world number.

Fuel costs are where the biggest savings are found. At the current UK average of around 134p per litre for unleaded petrol, a typical car doing 35 MPG costs roughly 15–16p per mile in fuel alone. By contrast, charging an EV at home on the Ofgem price cap rate of 24.5p per kWh works out to just 4–5p per mile for most modern electric cars. If you charge on a dedicated EV tariff like Octopus Go — which offers overnight rates as low as 7–9p per kWh — that drops further to under 2p per mile. For a driver covering 10,000 miles a year, that fuel saving alone can be worth £900–£1,400 annually.

Road tax (VED) is another meaningful saving. In 2026, petrol cars pay standard VED of £190 per year. Electric vehicles registered before April 2025 still enjoy zero VED, while those registered after pay £10 for the first year and £190 thereafter. Even at the standard rate, most EV drivers see a first-year saving of £180 on a new registration.

Servicing costs for EVs are substantially lower than petrol equivalents. With no oil changes, no spark plugs, no exhaust system, and fewer brake replacements thanks to regenerative braking, typical annual servicing costs fall from £300–£500 for a petrol car to £100–£200 for an EV. Brake pads and discs on EVs often last twice as long as on petrol cars.

Insurance is one area where EVs currently cost more. Premiums are typically 10–25% higher due to more expensive parts and specialist repair networks. This gap is narrowing rapidly, but it is worth including in your comparison — which this calculator does automatically. Edit every input field to match your situation: your annual mileage, your current car's MPG, your home electricity rate, and how often you use public rapid chargers. The more accurate your inputs, the more useful your personalised savings figure will be.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most UK drivers save between £800 and £1,500 per year on fuel alone by switching from petrol to electric. Add road tax savings of up to £190 per year and lower servicing costs — typically £150–£300 less annually — and total annual savings of £1,200–£2,000 are realistic for average mileage drivers covering around 8,000–10,000 miles a year. Higher-mileage drivers covering 15,000+ miles can save considerably more.
Under the Ofgem price cap in 2026, the unit rate for home electricity is approximately 24.5p per kWh. Many EV-specific tariffs such as Octopus Go, OVO Charge Anytime, or EDF GoElectric offer off-peak rates as low as 7p–9p per kWh overnight. At 7p/kWh, charging a 60 kWh battery from empty costs just £4.20 — enough for 200–250 miles in a modern EV. Choosing the right tariff is one of the single biggest steps you can take to maximise EV savings.
Break-even depends on the price premium you pay for an EV versus a comparable petrol car. With a typical £5,000–£8,000 premium and annual savings of £1,500, break-even usually falls between 3 and 6 years. Higher annual mileage shortens this considerably — a driver covering 15,000 miles a year may break even in under 4 years. Financing costs and depreciation also factor in; use our Total Cost of Ownership calculator for a full 5-year picture.
EV insurance in the UK is currently around 10–25% higher than equivalent petrol car policies, largely due to higher repair costs, specialist battery cover requirements, and a still-developing approved repair network. However, the gap is narrowing as insurers gain more data and more approved repairers enter the market. It is always worth comparing quotes from EV-specialist insurers such as Ageas, Direct Line, and Admiral alongside mainstream providers.
Over a 5-year ownership period, many EVs are cheaper in total cost of ownership than their petrol equivalents when you factor in lower fuel costs, road tax savings, reduced servicing bills, and strong residual values. The key variable is how much more you paid upfront. Second-hand EVs offer outstanding value — the purchase premium is lower while all the running cost savings remain.
