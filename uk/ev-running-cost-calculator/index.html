What Does It Really Cost to Run an EV in the UK?

Owning an electric vehicle in the UK is not just about the cost of charging. To understand the true cost of running an EV, you need to account for every recurring expense — charging electricity, insurance premiums, annual servicing, tyre replacement, and vehicle excise duty. This calculator brings all of those together into a single annual figure and a meaningful pence per mile cost.

Charging costs make up the largest portion of running costs and vary hugely depending on your setup. Home chargers at the standard Ofgem rate of 24.5p/kWh cost around 5–7p per mile for most EVs. An EV-specific overnight tariff like Octopus Go at 7p/kWh cuts that to under 2p per mile. Public rapid chargers at 65–79p/kWh push the cost above 15p per mile, similar to a reasonably efficient petrol car. Most UK EV owners do 80–90% of their charging at home, which keeps the overall charging cost per mile low.

Insurance currently adds a premium to EV ownership of around 10–25% versus a comparable petrol vehicle. For context, comprehensive car insurance for a mid-range EV typically costs £700–£1,200 per year depending on age, location, and no-claims history. This is a meaningful expense but is narrowing as the insurance market matures.

Servicing is one of the clearest advantages of EV ownership. With no oil to change, no timing belt, no spark plugs, and no exhaust system, annual EV servicing is significantly cheaper than petrol equivalents. A typical EV service in the UK costs £100–£250. Some manufacturers include servicing in the purchase price or subscription. Brakes also last longer due to regenerative braking — many EV owners go 60,000–80,000 miles before needing brake pad replacement.

Tyres are one area where EVs can cost slightly more. The additional weight of the battery pack and the instant torque delivery mean tyre wear can be higher, and EV-specific low rolling resistance tyres cost a premium. Budget £400–£600 per set, replaced slightly more frequently than on a petrol car. Nitrogen inflation and correct pressures help maximise tyre life.

Road tax (VED) for EVs registered before April 2025 remains £0 per year. Those registered after April 2025 pay a £10 first-year rate followed by the standard £190 annual VED from year two. EVs over £40,000 MSRP are also liable for the £620 luxury supplement for years 2–6, a significant addition for premium models like the Tesla Model Y and BMW iX.

Frequently Asked Questions

For a typical UK driver covering 8,000–10,000 miles a year, total EV running costs including charging, insurance, servicing, tyres, and road tax usually fall between £1,800 and £3,000 per year. Premium EVs with higher insurance premiums and larger tyres sit at the top of this range, while more affordable models like the MG4 or Nissan Leaf tend toward the lower end. Compare this to a comparable petrol car which typically costs £3,000–£4,500 per year to run.
Charging alone costs 2–7p per mile depending on whether you use an overnight home tariff or the standard Ofgem rate. When all running costs are added — insurance, servicing, tyres, and road tax — the all-in pence per mile figure typically falls between 18p and 30p for most UK EVs at average mileage. For context, an equivalent petrol car typically costs 30–45p per mile all-in, making the EV advantage clear at any mileage.
Annual EV servicing typically costs £100–£250 in the UK, compared to £300–£500 for a petrol car. EVs have no oil changes, no spark plugs, and rarely need brake pad replacement due to regenerative braking recapturing energy during deceleration. The main service items are tyres, cabin air filter replacement, brake fluid check every 2 years, wiper blades, and software updates. Tesla, Volkswagen, and Hyundai all offer competitive fixed-price service plans for their EV ranges.
EVs are heavier than equivalent petrol cars due to their battery packs, and they deliver instant torque which can accelerate tyre wear if driven aggressively. Studies suggest EV tyre wear can be 20–30% faster on average, though driving style is the dominant factor. EV-specific low rolling resistance tyres are recommended and typically cost £10–£30 more per tyre. Keeping tyres correctly inflated and rotating them regularly helps offset the additional wear significantly.
EVs registered before April 2025 pay £0 VED per year and retain this rate for the life of the vehicle. Those registered from April 2025 onwards pay £10 in year one and £190 per year thereafter — the same standard rate as petrol cars, following changes in the 2024 Spring Budget. Additionally, EVs with an MSRP over £40,000 are subject to the £620 annual luxury car supplement for years 2 through 6, which is a significant extra cost for buyers of premium models like the Tesla Model Y Performance, BMW i4, and Audi Q4 e-tron.
