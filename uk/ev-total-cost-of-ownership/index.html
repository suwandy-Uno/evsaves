Why Sticker Price Is Not the Full Story for EV Ownership

Walking into a dealership and comparing price tags between an EV and a petrol car will almost always make the petrol car look more attractive. But this misses the point entirely. The total cost of ownership (TCO) calculation accounts for every pound you spend — and save — over the full period you own the vehicle. For a significant proportion of UK drivers, EVs win this comparison convincingly.

What TCO includes: The calculation starts with the purchase price, then subtracts the estimated resale value at the end of the ownership period to give the depreciation cost. To this it adds all running costs over that period: electricity or fuel, insurance premiums, servicing bills, road tax (VED), and tyre replacement. If the vehicle is financed, the interest cost is added too. The result is the total real cost of ownership — the number that actually matters.

Depreciation is typically the largest single cost in a vehicle's TCO, often accounting for 40–50% of the total. New EVs in 2026 are depreciating at broadly similar rates to petrol equivalents after an earlier period of uncertainty. Popular models like the Tesla Model 3, Volkswagen ID.4, and Hyundai IONIQ 5 are showing strong 3-year residuals of 45–55% of original purchase price. Less popular or older-technology models may fall further. Second-hand EVs sidestep much of this depreciation loss, making used EV TCO particularly compelling.

Running cost savings are where EVs recoup ground rapidly. Over 5 years, a driver covering 10,000 miles annually saves approximately £6,000–£8,000 in fuel alone compared to a petrol car. Add servicing savings of £1,000–£1,500 over 5 years, road tax savings of up to £950 over the same period (for pre-April 2025 registrations), and the financial case for EVs over the medium term becomes compelling even for buyers who paid a £5,000–£8,000 premium upfront.

Finance costs deserve careful attention. Manufacturer-subsidised 0% finance deals, increasingly common for EVs as brands try to drive adoption, make TCO comparisons strongly favour EVs. However, a standard 8% APR personal loan on a higher purchase price can add several thousand pounds to the true cost. Always calculate the total amount repayable on any finance deal before signing.

Adjust all the inputs in the calculator to match your specific situation — purchase price, expected mileage, finance rate, and anticipated resale value. The more accurate your inputs, the more reliable your 5-year TCO comparison will be.
