UK EV Road Tax (VED) Explained: What Changed in 2025 and What It Means for You

Vehicle Excise Duty — commonly known as road tax — has been one of the clearest financial advantages of driving an electric vehicle in the UK. But significant changes introduced from April 2025 have altered the picture for new EV buyers. Understanding exactly what applies to your vehicle is important for calculating your true ownership costs.

EVs registered before 1 April 2025 continue to enjoy complete VED exemption — £0 per year for the life of the vehicle, regardless of when the rules changed. This exemption was baked in at the point of registration and applies permanently. If you own or are considering buying a used EV registered before this date, you retain the full VED saving of £190 per year compared to a standard petrol car. Over 5 years, that is a saving of £950.

EVs registered from 1 April 2025 are treated differently under the changes announced in the 2024 Spring Budget. New electric cars registered from this date pay a first-year rate of just £10 — a significant saving versus the CO2-based first-year VED petrol cars pay, which ranges from £220 to over £2,700 depending on emissions. From year two onwards, however, EVs registered after April 2025 pay the same standard annual rate as petrol cars — £190 per year in 2026.

The luxury car supplement — sometimes called the expensive car supplement — is an additional £620 per year that applies to any vehicle (petrol or electric) with an original list price over £40,000. It is charged from year two through year six of the vehicle's registration. From April 2025, this surcharge now applies to EVs as well as petrol and diesel vehicles. This is a meaningful additional cost for buyers of popular EVs including the Tesla Model 3 Long Range (around £42,000), BMW i4, Hyundai IONIQ 6, and Mercedes EQA. Combined with the standard VED, affected EV owners in years 2–6 pay £810 per year in road tax — more than most petrol car owners.

Comparing EV versus petrol VED over 5 years: For an EV registered before April 2025, the saving over a standard petrol car is £190 per year, or £950 over 5 years. For a new EV registered after April 2025 under £40,000 MSRP, the 5-year saving shrinks to £180 (year one only). For a new EV over £40,000, there is no VED saving in years 2–6 compared to the equivalent petrol car, and both pay the same standard £190 rate. The luxury supplement applies equally to both.
