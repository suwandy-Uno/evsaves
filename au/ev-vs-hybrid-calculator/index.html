EV vs Hybrid in Australia: What Does the Data Say?

The Toyota RAV4 Hybrid and Camry Hybrid are Australia's best-selling passenger vehicles — hybrids are enormously popular here. But as EV prices drop and charging infrastructure improves, more Australians are questioning whether a hybrid still makes financial sense versus a full battery electric vehicle.

On fuel costs alone, an EV wins decisively. A typical hybrid doing 5.5L/100km at $1.89/L costs $1,040 per year in fuel for 10,000km. The equivalent distance in an EV at 5.8km/kWh and 32c/kWh costs $552 — a saving of nearly $490 per year just on energy, before any other factors.

For longer-distance drivers covering 20,000km per year, the gap doubles. The hybrid costs $2,079 in fuel; the EV costs $1,103. The annual energy saving of $976 begins to close the higher purchase price gap within 5–7 years for most scenarios.

Where hybrids still have an edge: long regional routes with limited fast charging. While Australia's charging network is improving rapidly (NRMA, Chargefox, Evie Networks), coverage is still sparse on some remote routes. A hybrid eliminates range anxiety on an Alice Springs to Darwin drive in a way that no EV can match today. If your driving regularly includes isolated regional routes, the practical arguments for a hybrid remain strong.

The FBT exemption advantage: Australia's Fringe Benefits Tax exemption for novated lease EVs (and eligible PHEVs) changes the financial equation significantly for employees. Under a novated lease, an EV can be acquired with pre-tax salary, creating savings of $3,000–$10,000+ for higher earners. This benefit does not apply to self-charging hybrids — only to battery electric vehicles and plug-in hybrids.
