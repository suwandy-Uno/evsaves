Australia EV Frequently Asked Questions

At a typical Australian household electricity rate of around 32¢/kWh, a full charge of a 75kWh battery costs approximately $24 AUD. For an average Australian driver covering 13,000km per year in an EV with a real-world efficiency of around 6km/kWh, annual home charging costs come to roughly $693 AUD. Electricity rates vary significantly by state — South Australia pays more (around 42¢/kWh) while Western Australia pays less (around 28¢/kWh). Use our EV Charging Cost Calculator to set your state's rate.

Stamp duty exemptions and concessions for EVs vary by state and are changing rapidly. As of 2026, ACT offers a full stamp duty exemption for eligible EVs, while NSW, Victoria and Queensland have wound back earlier concessions. South Australia, Western Australia and Tasmania each have their own rules and thresholds. The savings can be substantial — $1,000 to $3,000+ on a typical EV purchase. Use our EV Stamp Duty Calculator to check your state's current rules.

Australian conditions — long distances, hot weather, and a still-developing public charging network — favour EVs with larger battery packs and efficient thermal management. The Tesla Model Y (77kWh, up to 533km WLTP range) and BYD Atto 3 (60.5kWh, up to 480km WLTP range) are among the most popular choices for their range and charging network coverage. The MG4 offers the best value for shorter-range urban drivers. Our model-specific calculators show running costs for all of these under Australian prices.

With Australia's high solar irradiance and falling solar panel costs, many EV owners can meet 40–80% of their annual charging from rooftop solar, reducing their effective cost per kWh to near zero during the day. A typical 6.6kW system in Sydney can generate enough excess power to cover 10,000–15,000km of EV driving per year. Our Solar EV Charging Calculator estimates your solar charging coverage and annual saving based on your system size and location.

At $1.89/litre petrol and 32¢/kWh home electricity, a driver covering 13,000km/year in a 7L/100km petrol car spends about $1,716 on fuel. The equivalent EV (at 18kWh/100km real-world) costs about $748 on home electricity — a saving of roughly $968/year. Add servicing savings of $300–$500 per year (EVs have fewer moving parts and no oil changes), and total annual savings typically exceed $1,200.
