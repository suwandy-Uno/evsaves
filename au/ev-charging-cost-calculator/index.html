EV Charging Costs in Australia: Home, Public and Solar

Charging an EV in Australia is significantly cheaper than fuelling a petrol car — but your actual cost depends heavily on where and when you charge. Understanding the difference between home charging, workplace charging, and public fast charging is essential for estimating your real-world EV running costs.

Home charging is the cheapest and most convenient option for most Australians. The national average electricity rate sits around 32 cents per kWh in 2026, though this varies meaningfully by state. NSW households typically pay 28–36c/kWh depending on their retailer and plan; Victorian households enjoy 26–32c/kWh; Queensland rates range from 25–30c/kWh; and Western Australian households on the Synergy tariff pay around 30c/kWh. At 32c/kWh, fully charging a 75 kWh battery from empty costs around $24 and delivers approximately 400–450 km of real-world range.

Smart charging plans can reduce home charging costs further. Several Australian electricity retailers now offer EV-specific tariffs with cheaper overnight rates. AGL's EV Plan, Origin's EV tariff, and Amber Electric's spot-price model allow savvy EV owners to charge when electricity is cheapest — often between midnight and 6am when wholesale prices dip as low as 5–10c/kWh. Time-of-use plans typically offer off-peak rates of 15–22c/kWh, cutting your charging bill roughly in half compared to the flat rate.

Public charging networks in Australia have expanded rapidly. The major networks include:

  • Chargefox — Australia's largest public charging network with over 700 charging stations. Ultra-rapid 350kW chargers are priced around 60c/kWh for non-members; a $10/month Chargefox subscription reduces this to around 45c/kWh.
  • NRMA EV Charging — The NRMA has invested heavily in fast charging infrastructure across NSW and the ACT, with rates from 40–55c/kWh depending on charger speed.
  • AmpCharge — Ampol's growing charging network at Ampol service stations, offering rates from 49–59c/kWh at 50kW and 150kW chargers.
  • Tesla Supercharger — Tesla's proprietary network (now open to non-Tesla vehicles via the Magic Dock adaptor at select sites) charges around 52–58c/kWh. Tesla owners pay slightly less, especially with a monthly membership.
  • ChargePoint, Evie Networks — Additional networks with competitive pricing, often priced at 40–55c/kWh.

At 55c/kWh, a public fast charge costs around $8.80 per 100km for a typical EV — still well below the $16+ per 100km cost of petrol, but considerably more than home charging at around $5.12 per 100km.

Solar charging is a game-changer for Australian EV owners. Australia has the highest per-capita solar uptake in the world, with over 3.5 million residential solar installations. A typical 6.6kW solar system generates 25–30 kWh per day in most Australian capitals, easily enough to cover typical daily driving. When you charge directly from your solar panels, the effective cost of charging is the solar self-consumption rate — essentially what you would have received from your feed-in tariff (now typically just 5–10c/kWh). Many Australians with solar are charging their EVs for less than $1 per 100km.

Workplace charging, where available, often provides free or heavily subsidised charging as an employee benefit. This is especially common in larger corporations and government departments, which are rapidly adding EV charging infrastructure to company car parks.
