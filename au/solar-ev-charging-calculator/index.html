Solar + EV in Australia: The Perfect Combination

Australia is the world leader in residential solar installations with over 3.5 million rooftop systems generating clean electricity. When you combine solar panels with an electric vehicle, the financial case becomes exceptional — potentially driving on near-free energy for much of the year.

A typical 6.6kW solar system in most Australian states generates 24–32kWh per day — more than enough to fully charge most EVs overnight (when paired with a home battery) or during the day (with a smart charger set to charge during solar production hours). In Queensland and Western Australia, solar generation is even higher due to superior irradiance.

Self-consumption matters more now. Feed-in tariffs (FiTs) have dropped dramatically across all states — from 44c in early incentive schemes to 5–8c/kWh today in most areas. That means using your solar energy to charge your EV yourself (at a "cost" of 5–8c/kWh) is far better than exporting it for 6c and buying grid power at 32c/kWh. The financial case for direct solar EV charging has never been stronger.

Smart charging strategies: Zappi chargers (popular in AU) automatically divert excess solar to your EV when generation exceeds home load. Apps from Wallbox, Fronius (Ohmpilot), and SolarEdge also offer EV solar divert features. If you're not home during the day, a home battery (such as a Tesla Powerwall or Sungrow SBH) can store solar energy for overnight charging.

In sunny states like Queensland and WA, an EV owner with a 6.6kW solar system could realistically cover 80–100% of their annual EV charging energy from the sun — reducing their effective per-km energy cost to near zero. Even in cloudy Victoria or Tasmania, 40–60% solar coverage is achievable for average driving patterns.

Frequently Asked Questions

For 15,000km per year at 5.5km/kWh, you need approximately 2,727kWh of electricity annually for your EV. A 3kW solar panel addition can cover most of this in most Australian cities, producing 4,000–5,000kWh annually. A standard 6.6kW system (which most homes already have) produces 8,000–12,000kWh/year — well more than enough for an EV plus household needs.
Feed-in tariffs vary by state and retailer: NSW 5–8c/kWh, VIC 4–6c/kWh (via the VDO minimum), QLD 5–6c/kWh (Energex/Ergon area), SA 5–7c/kWh, WA 2.25–10c/kWh (Synergy/Horizon). Premium rates from early solar programs have now expired. Always check your current retailer contract for your exact FiT.

If you're not home, a home battery (Powerwall, Sungrow SBH, etc.) can store midday solar for evening charging. Many Australian EV owners set timers to charge during peak solar hours (10am–3pm).
The financial payback on a home battery purely for EV charging is typically 7–12 years at current battery prices ($12,000–$18,000 installed for a Powerwall 3). However, when you factor in grid independence, blackout protection, and the total household energy offset, many Australians find the combination worthwhile — especially in states with rising electricity prices and low FiTs.

The financial case is strong everywhere — the main variable is how much of your EV charging you can meet from self-generated solar versus grid power.
