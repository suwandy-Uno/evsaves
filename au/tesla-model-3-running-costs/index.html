Tesla Model 3 Running Costs: Australia's Most Popular Premium EV

The Tesla Model 3 remains one of the best-selling electric vehicles in Australia, and for good reason. Its combination of performance, range, technology, and a comprehensive fast-charging network make it a compelling choice for Australian drivers. But what does it actually cost to own and run one in 2026?

Australian pricing: The Tesla Model 3 RWD (Standard Range) starts at approximately $59,900 drive-away in Australia, the Long Range RWD at around $73,900, and the Performance variant at $82,900. These prices place the Model 3 comfortably below the luxury car tax threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles (~$89,332), meaning standard stamp duty and LCT implications are minimal for the RWD and Long Range variants.

Efficiency and charging costs: The Tesla Model 3 RWD achieves a real-world efficiency of approximately 6.3 km/kWh in Australian conditions — equivalent to about 15.9 kWh/100km. At the home charging rate of 32c/kWh, this works out to roughly $5.07 per 100km. For a typical 15,000km Australian driver, annual electricity costs are approximately $760 per year charged at home.

Tesla Supercharger network in Australia: Tesla's Supercharger network has expanded significantly across Australia, with over 130 Supercharger stations covering all major cities and key highway routes. The network includes V3 Superchargers capable of delivering up to 250kW for compatible Model 3 variants. Tesla charges approximately 52–58c/kWh at Australian Supercharger stations. For road trips, budget an additional 15–25% on charging costs compared to home-only charging.

Servicing: Tesla's servicing model is unique in Australia. The Model 3 has no fixed service intervals — instead, the vehicle monitors its own condition and alerts you when something needs attention. Tesla recommends an annual inspection (approximately $180–$250 at a Tesla Service Centre), plus periodic items like cabin air filter ($80), wiper blades ($60), and tyre rotation. Total annual servicing costs average around $200–$300 — far below the $800–$1,200 typical for a comparable premium petrol sedan like a BMW 3 Series or Mercedes C-Class.

Insurance: Comprehensive insurance for a Tesla Model 3 in Australia typically ranges from $1,200–$1,800 per year depending on driver profile, location, and insurer. Tesla Insurance has launched in Australia and offers competitive pricing for Tesla owners. Specialty EV insurers and mainstream insurers (NRMA, AAMI, Budget Direct) all cover the Model 3. CTP insurance is included in registration in most states.

Novated lease appeal: The Tesla Model 3 is among the most novated-leased vehicles in Australia following the FBT exemption. Under a novated lease, the RWD and Long Range models fall below the LCT threshold, maximising the tax benefit. Fleet managers and salary packaging providers report the Model 3 as their most requested EV for novated leases. For someone on a $100,000+ salary, the after-tax cost of a novated Model 3 can be as low as $600–$800 per month all-inclusive.

Variant comparison: The RWD (rear-wheel drive) Model 3 suits urban and suburban drivers covering typical distances. The Long Range RWD offers around 620km WLTP range and is ideal for interstate travellers and regional drivers. The Performance variant adds a front motor for dual-motor AWD and 0–100km/h in 3.3 seconds, with a slight efficiency trade-off to around 5.8 km/kWh.
