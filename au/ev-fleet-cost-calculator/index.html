Why Australian Businesses Are Switching to EV Fleets

The Fringe Benefits Tax exemption for electric vehicles — introduced in November 2022 — has been a game changer for Australian business fleets. Under the exemption, eligible zero-emission vehicles provided as a benefit to employees via salary packaging or novated lease arrangements are exempt from FBT, creating savings of thousands of dollars per vehicle per year for businesses and employees alike.

The FBT exemption mechanics: Normally, an employer providing a car to an employee must pay FBT at 47% on the deemed value of the benefit. Under the EV exemption, this liability is eliminated for eligible BEVs (and PHEVs until April 2025, check current ATO guidance). For a business providing 10 EVs to employees, eliminating FBT can save $15,000–$40,000 per year in total tax cost — a compelling fleet incentive.

Fuel cost savings are the other major driver. At $1.89/L for petrol and a fleet average of 10L/100km, each petrol fleet vehicle costs approximately $3,780 per year in fuel for 20,000km. An equivalent EV at 5.5km/kWh and commercial electricity at 28c/kWh costs approximately $1,018 per year — a saving of $2,762 per vehicle annually. For a 10-vehicle fleet, that's $27,620 per year in fuel savings.

Servicing costs also drop significantly. EVs have fewer service intervals and no oil, filter, spark plug, or exhaust maintenance requirements. RACQ data suggests EV servicing costs are 40–50% lower than equivalent petrol vehicles. For a 10-vehicle fleet covering 20,000km/year each, this can represent an additional $2,000–$5,000 in annual savings.

Depot charging infrastructure is a key consideration. Installing 7kW or 22kW chargers at a depot or office car park typically costs $500–$1,500 per bay. Many state governments and ARENA (Australian Renewable Energy Agency) have offered grants for business EV charging infrastructure — check the business.gov.au grants finder for current opportunities.
