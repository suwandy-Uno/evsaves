Tesla Model Y: Australia's Most Popular EV Running Costs

The Tesla Model Y was Australia's best-selling electric vehicle in 2024 and 2025, and it continues to dominate the EV market in 2026. As an all-electric SUV with genuine five-seat (or seven-seat) capability, a 500+ km real-world range, and access to Tesla's comprehensive Supercharger network, the Model Y appeals strongly to Australian families. But what does it actually cost to run one?

Australian pricing: The Tesla Model Y RWD starts at approximately $65,900 drive-away, the Long Range AWD at around $78,900, and the Performance AWD variant at approximately $87,900. These prices place all three variants near or slightly above the fuel-efficient LCT threshold, so buyers of the Performance model should check current LCT implications. The updated "Juniper" Model Y (released in Australia in 2024) brought refreshed styling, improved interior quality, and enhanced efficiency.

Efficiency: The Model Y RWD achieves approximately 5.7 km/kWh in real-world Australian conditions — equivalent to roughly 17.5 kWh/100km. As an SUV, it uses slightly more energy than the Model 3 sedan due to its larger size and higher weight. At 32c/kWh home charging, this equates to around $5.60 per 100km, or approximately $840 per year for a 15,000km driver.

Family use case: The Model Y's popularity in Australia is driven primarily by family buyers replacing SUVs like the Toyota RAV4, Mazda CX-5, or Hyundai Tucson. At comparable overall running costs (including the charging cost advantage), the Model Y competes very effectively on a 5-year total cost basis against mid-sized petrol SUVs. The Long Range AWD variant, in particular, appeals to families who need genuine outback capability and the confidence of all-wheel drive for wet or unpaved roads.

Insurance: Comprehensive insurance for the Tesla Model Y in Australia typically runs $1,400–$1,900 per year — slightly higher than the Model 3 due to the higher vehicle value. Families with additional named drivers may see higher premiums. Tesla Insurance is available in Australia and can offer competitive pricing, particularly for drivers with clean driving records. Multiple insurer quotes through comparison platforms are recommended annually.

Servicing: Like the Model 3, the Model Y has no fixed service intervals. Annual inspection costs are approximately $200–$280 at Tesla Service Centres. Common annual costs include cabin air filter, wiper blades, tyre rotation, and periodic brake fluid replacement. The seven-seat variant has identical powertrain servicing requirements to the five-seat version. Total annual servicing costs are typically $200–$350.

Buy vs novated lease: The Model Y is very popular under novated leases in Australia, particularly for employees earning $90,000+. Under an FBT-exempt novated lease, the pre-tax cost of running a Model Y (including electricity, insurance, rego, and servicing) can make the net monthly out-of-pocket significantly lower than a post-tax car loan. Many Australian salary packaging providers quote Model Y novated leases at an effective post-tax cost of $700–$1,000 per month all-inclusive for the RWD variant — comparable to leasing a mid-range petrol SUV.

Charging infrastructure for families: Most Model Y owners in Australia install a 7.2kW home wall charger (Wallbox, NHP, or similar) for overnight charging. The Model Y's 75kWh battery charges from 20–80% in approximately 6 hours on a home wallbox — perfect for overnight top-ups. The Supercharger network's coverage of family road trip routes (Sydney–Brisbane via the Gold Coast, Melbourne–Adelaide, Perth–Bunbury) makes interstate travel straightforward.

Fuel savings of $1,500–$2,500 per year versus a petrol RAV4-sized SUV, combined with lower servicing costs, offset much of the higher purchase price. Add the FBT exemption for eligible novated lease buyers and the financial case becomes even stronger. The Supercharger network's reliability and coverage also makes it more practical than many competing EVs for long-distance family travel.
Real-world range in Australian conditions varies by variant and conditions. The Model Y RWD achieves approximately 430–480km in typical city and highway mixed driving at moderate speeds. The Long Range AWD achieves 520–570km. In hot Queensland summers, range can drop 5–10% due to air conditioning load; in cold Victorian winters the reduction is typically 10–15%. Driving at 110km/h highway speeds reduces range significantly compared to the official WLTP figures.
