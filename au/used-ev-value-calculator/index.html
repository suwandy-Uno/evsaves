Valuing a Used EV in Australia

Australia's used EV market is growing rapidly but remains relatively immature compared to Europe or North America. This means used EV prices can be less predictable, and battery health is an even more critical factor in determining value. Understanding how to value a second-hand EV can save you thousands — whether you're buying or selling.

RedBook and Glass's are the standard trade valuation guides used by Australian dealers, similar to the US Kelley Blue Book. Both now cover EVs with dedicated sections. Carsales, CarExpert, and Drive.com.au also publish used EV market reports that reflect real transaction prices. These sources are your best check against our calculator's estimate.

Battery health is the single most important determinant of a used EV's value in Australia. A Tesla Model 3 with 80% battery health is worth significantly less than one with 92% health, even with the same age and mileage. Most modern EVs (Tesla, BYD, Hyundai) display current battery capacity in their apps or settings menus. For older Nissan Leafs, the LeafSpy app and an OBD2 Bluetooth dongle provides State of Health data.

Climate impact on depreciation is more pronounced in Australia than in cooler countries. EVs in Queensland, WA, and NT that have been regularly exposed to temperatures above 35°C may show faster battery degradation than equivalent vehicles from cooler states. This can reduce market value by 5–15% compared to a similar vehicle from Victoria or Tasmania.

Nissan Leaf caveat: Early Australian Leaf imports (2014–2019) had passive thermal management and are prone to faster capacity loss in hot climates. A 2016 Leaf in Queensland may have 65–75% battery health, which dramatically reduces its market value and practical usefulness. Always check the health bar in the Leaf's dashboard or use LeafSpy before purchasing any older Leaf.
