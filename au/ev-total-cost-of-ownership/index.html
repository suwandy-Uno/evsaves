Understanding EV Total Cost of Ownership in Australia

When comparing an electric vehicle to a petrol car in Australia, the sticker price is just the beginning. The total cost of ownership (TCO) over 5 years tells a very different story — one that increasingly favours EVs as purchase prices fall and running cost savings accumulate. This calculator breaks down every major cost item to give you the complete financial picture.

Purchase price and on-road costs: The average new EV sold in Australia in 2026 is priced around $55,000–$65,000, though the market ranges from sub-$40,000 (MG4) to over $200,000 (Mercedes EQS, Porsche Taycan). On-road costs include dealer delivery ($1,000–$2,500), CTP insurance (included in rego in most states), and stamp duty. Stamp duty is the most variable item — in Victoria it was abolished for EVs in 2024; in NSW it's $3 per $200 of vehicle value above $45,000; the ACT has zero stamp duty on EVs; QLD has standard duty but a $3,000 rebate. These differences can add or subtract $1,000–$4,000 to the upfront cost depending on where you register the vehicle.

Luxury Car Tax (LCT): Vehicles priced above the fuel-efficient LCT threshold (approximately $89,332 in 2025–26) attract a 33% tax on the amount above the threshold. This significantly inflates the effective purchase price of premium EVs. For example, a Tesla Model S priced at $150,000 would attract LCT of approximately $20,000. The threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles (including most EVs) is substantially higher than the standard threshold, providing some relief for the mid-range EV market.

Depreciation: EV depreciation has been a hot topic in Australia. Early adopter EVs (notably the Nissan Leaf and some early Hyundai models) depreciated steeply due to battery range anxiety and rapid technology progression. More recent EVs — particularly Tesla models — have shown surprisingly strong residual values, with some used Teslas retaining 60–70% of their value after 3 years. BYD and MG models are newer to the Australian market and their long-term residual values are less established, though analysts generally expect 40–55% retention after 5 years.

Running costs over 5 years: At 15,000 km per year and home charging at 32c/kWh, electricity costs approximately $3,800 over 5 years. Add $1,500–$2,000 in servicing, $5,500–$7,500 in insurance, $1,500–$3,000 in registration, and $1,000–$1,500 in tyres. Total 5-year running costs: approximately $13,000–$17,000 for a mainstream EV.

Novated lease vs outright purchase: The FBT exemption makes novated leasing particularly attractive in Australia for eligible EVs. An employee salary-sacrificing a $65,000 EV under a novated lease on a $120,000 salary can effectively reduce the real post-tax cost of the vehicle by 25–40% compared to an outright purchase. This is because novated leases include running costs (fuel/charging, rego, insurance, servicing) in the pre-tax salary package, all of which become FBT-free for eligible EVs. The net effect is that the government effectively subsidises a significant portion of the EV's total cost of ownership through the tax system.

For high-mileage drivers (20,000–30,000 km/year), the financial case for an EV over 5 years is even more compelling. The running cost savings compound with distance, and the higher utilisation means the upfront price premium is spread over more kilometres driven.
