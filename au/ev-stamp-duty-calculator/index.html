EV Stamp Duty in Australia: State-by-State Guide

Stamp duty on vehicle purchases in Australia is administered by each state and territory, meaning the rules and concessions for electric vehicles vary enormously depending on where you live. Some states have abolished stamp duty on EVs entirely; others offer partial rebates; and some apply standard stamp duty with no concession at all.

Victoria abolished stamp duty on new EVs priced below the luxury car tax threshold ($76,950 in 2025–26) from 2024. This saves buyers approximately $2,000–$2,500 on a $50,000 EV. Used EVs registered in Victoria for the first time also qualify.

ACT has the most generous policy — zero stamp duty on new EVs and motorcycles, and has offered this since 2021. EV buyers in Canberra save $1,500–$4,000+ depending on vehicle price.

NSW applies a reduced stamp duty rate for EVs and PHEVs: $3 per $100 of value for EVs versus $3–$5 per $100 for standard vehicles depending on the price. On a $55,000 EV, this equates to saving around $400–$700.

Queensland does not exempt EVs from stamp duty but has offered a $3,000 rebate for new EVs under $58,000 (subject to income test). Check the Queensland government website for current status as rebate schemes have changed.

South Australia offered a $3,000 rebate for new BEVs and hydrogen vehicles under $68,750 (scheme now closed). WA has offered a 10% stamp duty reduction. Tasmania offers no specific EV stamp duty concession. Northern Territory applies standard rates.

Important: Incentive schemes are updated frequently. Always verify your state's current position directly with the State Revenue Office before purchasing. The calculator below gives you a general estimate using configurable rates.
