How Much Power Does Your Modification Add?

Horsepower gains from car modifications vary enormously depending on the type of modification, your starting power level, and the supporting modifications in place. Understanding realistic expectations prevents expensive disappointment.

ECU remap / tune: The single most cost-effective modification for most turbocharged petrol and diesel engines. A Stage 1 remap (no hardware changes required) typically adds 15–30% on a diesel and 10–20% on a petrol turbo engine. On naturally aspirated engines, gains are modest — typically 2–8%. The gain percentage depends heavily on how conservative the factory tune is.

Cold air intake: On its own, a performance intake adds 5–15 hp on most engines. Gains are more significant when combined with a remap. The improvement comes from reducing intake air temperature and restriction. The cooler, denser air allows the engine to burn more fuel, producing more power.

Performance exhaust system: A cat-back exhaust system adds 5–15 hp on most engines by reducing exhaust backpressure. A full turbo-back system (downpipe included) can add 20–40 hp on a turbocharged engine. Note that downpipe modifications may affect emissions compliance — check local regulations.

Intercooler upgrade: On a turbocharged engine, a larger front-mount intercooler reduces charge air temperature, allowing for more aggressive timing and boost. Gains of 20–60 hp are realistic when combined with a remap. Essential for sustained power on hot days or hard driving.

Turbocharger upgrade: A larger turbo can dramatically increase peak power but typically trades low-end response for peak output. Combined with fuel system upgrades and a remap, gains of 50–200 hp over stock are achievable with a correctly sized turbo upgrade.

Always verify gains on a rolling road (dyno) before and after modifications to get accurate, measured results rather than manufacturer claims.

Frequently Asked Questions

An ECU remap/tune consistently delivers the best power-per-pound. On a turbocharged diesel, a Stage 1 remap costs £300–£600 and adds 40–80 hp and significant torque. On a petrol turbo, gains of 30–60 hp for a similar cost are typical. No other modification approaches this efficiency on a turbocharged engine.
BHP (brake horsepower) is the traditional UK/AU measurement. HP (horsepower) is the US standard. PS (Pferdestärke, from German) is the metric equivalent. For practical purposes: 1 PS = 0.9863 HP ≈ 0.9863 BHP. The differences are small — a car quoted at 200 PS, 200 HP, or 200 BHP are all very similar. Our calculator uses generic "hp" — apply the same percentage gain regardless of which unit your car is rated in.
Manufacturer figures are typically measured at the flywheel (engine output). Dyno (rolling road) figures measure at the wheels and are lower due to drivetrain losses — typically 10–15% for FWD, 12–17% for RWD, 15–20% for AWD. A car quoted at 200 bhp might show 165–175 bhp on a rolling road. This is normal. Compare before/after dyno figures on the same setup for accurate modification gains.
A Stage 2 tune (requiring hardware changes) typically needs: an uprated intercooler, a performance air intake (panel filter or cold air intake), a high-flow downpipe (decat or sports cat), and often a stronger clutch. The tune is then optimised for these components working together. Stage 2 might add 60–120 hp over stock on a popular turbocharged hatchback platform.
