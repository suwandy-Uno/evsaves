How to Value a Used EV in the United States

The US used EV market has evolved significantly. Tesla vehicles have historically retained value exceptionally well — iSeekCars data shows the Model Y retaining 65–70% of its value at 3 years, outperforming most ICE vehicles. However, non-Tesla EVs have generally depreciated more sharply, partly because the IRA tax credit makes new EVs relatively more attractive.

The used EV tax credit under the IRA offers up to $4,000 (or 30% of purchase price, whichever is lower) for qualifying used EVs under $25,000 purchased from a licensed dealer. This has created a floor for used EV prices in the $20,000–$25,000 range — buyers seeking the credit won't pay above $25,000, and the credit itself supports pricing near that threshold. If you're selling a used EV, pricing just under $25,000 may attract more buyers seeking the credit.

Battery health is paramount. Use official tools where available: Tesla's energy data and service centre battery reports; Hyundai/Kia's GDS diagnostic system at a dealer; Nissan Leaf's LeafSpy app (OBD2 dongle required). A battery at 75% capacity represents a meaningful range reduction and should command a significantly lower price than one at 90%.

Hot climate impact: EVs operated in Arizona, Nevada, Texas, and Florida may show faster battery degradation than equivalent vehicles from cooler climates. This is particularly relevant for early Nissan Leafs (passive cooling) and older Chevy Bolts. A Leaf from Phoenix may have 70% capacity while an identical age/mileage Leaf from Seattle might be at 85%.

Cross-reference our estimate with Kelley Blue Book (kbb.com), Edmunds (edmunds.com/used-cars), CarGurus, and Autotrader for real current market prices. The official IRS list at fueleconomy.gov shows which vehicles qualify for the $4,000 used credit.

Tesla's Supercharger network, frequent OTA updates that improve performance, and strong brand loyalty support resale prices. Among non-Tesla EVs, Hyundai Ioniq 5/6 and Kia EV6 have shown better retention than older EVs. Chevy Bolt and Nissan Leaf (especially older models) depreciate more steeply due to rapid model obsolescence and the $25,000 used credit threshold pulling prices down.

CPO EVs from Tesla, Hyundai, Kia, and other manufacturers offer extended warranty coverage (typically 4–5 years powertrain including battery), reconditioning inspection, and in some cases roadside assistance.
