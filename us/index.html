US EV Frequently Asked Questions

At the US national average electricity rate of around 16¢/kWh, a full charge of a 75kWh battery costs approximately $12. For an average American driver covering 14,000 miles per year in an EV rated at 3.5 miles/kWh (EPA), annual home charging costs come to roughly $640. Electricity rates vary significantly by state — Louisiana and Idaho are among the cheapest (around 10–11¢/kWh) while California and Hawaii are among the most expensive (28–42¢/kWh). Use our EV Charging Cost Calculator to enter your state's rate.

The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) provides up to $7,500 in federal tax credits for qualifying new EVs and up to $4,000 for used EVs. Eligibility depends on three things: the buyer's income (under $150,000 AGI for single filers, $300,000 for joint filers), the vehicle's MSRP (under $55,000 for cars, $80,000 for SUVs/trucks), and where the battery and components are manufactured. Not all EVs qualify — check our EV Tax Credit Calculator to see if your chosen vehicle meets the 2026 requirements.

At $3.45/gallon gas and 16¢/kWh home electricity, a driver covering 14,000 miles per year in a 30mpg gas car spends about $1,610 on gas. The equivalent EV (at 3.5 miles/kWh EPA) costs about $640 on home electricity — a saving of approximately $970/year. Add maintenance savings of $400–$800 per year (no oil changes, fewer brake replacements due to regenerative braking), and total annual savings often exceed $1,400. Use our EV vs Gas Calculator to personalise this with your own mileage and state prices.

For most EV owners, yes — a Level 2 (240V) home charger is one of the best investments you can make alongside an EV purchase. It charges at 15–50 miles of range per hour (vs 3–5 miles/hour on a standard 120V outlet), meaning a typical overnight charge fully replenishes even a 300-mile battery. Installation costs range from $400 to $1,200 depending on your electrical panel and cable run, and the 30% federal tax credit (Form 5695) reduces the net cost further. Use our Home Charger Installation Calculator to estimate your payback period.

Over 5 years, many EV owners in the US come out ahead of an equivalent gas car even after accounting for the higher purchase price — particularly when the $7,500 federal tax credit is applied. A 2026 Tesla Model Y (after tax credit) costs approximately $2,000–$5,000 more up front than a comparable Toyota RAV4. But over 5 years, fuel and maintenance savings of $7,000–$10,000 make the EV the cheaper option overall for most drivers. Results vary significantly by state electricity price and annual mileage. Use our TCO Calculator to run your specific numbers.
