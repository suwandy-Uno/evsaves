Solar + EV in the US: A Powerful Combination

The combination of rooftop solar and an electric vehicle is one of the most financially compelling setups for US homeowners. With the Inflation Reduction Act's 30% federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) on solar systems available through 2032, and rapidly falling panel prices, payback periods on residential solar are now typically 6–10 years in most US states — and significantly shorter in high-electricity-cost states like California, Hawaii, and New York.

The federal solar ITC allows you to deduct 30% of the cost of a solar system from your federal taxes. A $20,000 system generates a $6,000 tax credit — reducing the net cost to $14,000. Many states also offer additional solar incentives, rebates, and tax credits. The Database of State Incentives for Renewables & Efficiency (dsireusa.org) lists all current state programs.

Net metering allows solar homeowners to sell excess generation back to their utility at or near retail rates in many states. However, California's NEM 3.0 (implemented 2023) dramatically reduced export rates, shifting the calculus toward self-consumption. In states with robust net metering (most of the country), exporting solar energy at retail rates remains valuable. In states moving toward NEM 3.0-style structures, using solar to charge your EV directly (instead of exporting) becomes more important.

Time-of-use tariffs are another consideration. Many utilities charge more for electricity during peak hours (4–9pm) and less during off-peak hours. With a smart EV charger and a solar-plus-battery setup, you can charge your EV cheaply overnight while your battery, charged by midday sun, handles peak-hour household loads. This "rate arbitrage" can save an additional $300–$600/year on electricity bills in states with aggressive TOU pricing.

The best states for solar EV synergy: California (high electricity costs despite NEM 3.0), Texas (deregulated market with cheap off-peak rates), Arizona (premium solar resource), Florida (good irradiance, net metering), and the Northeast (high electricity prices make solar savings larger).
