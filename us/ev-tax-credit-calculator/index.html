Understanding the $7,500 Federal EV Tax Credit

The Clean Vehicle Credit under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) offers up to $7,500 for qualifying new electric vehicles and up to $4,000 for qualifying used EVs. However, the amount you can actually use is limited by your federal tax liability for the year — you cannot receive the credit as a refund if it exceeds what you owe in taxes.

Point-of-sale transfer (from 2024): Since January 2024, you can transfer the EV tax credit to the dealer at the time of purchase, effectively reducing the purchase price by up to $7,500 even if your tax liability doesn't cover the full amount. The dealer then claims the credit from the IRS. This is now the most common way US buyers use the credit. Not all dealers are enrolled in the transfer program — confirm with your dealer before purchase.

Eligibility requirements for the full $7,500 credit: The vehicle must be assembled in North America (check the IRS VIN decoder at fueleconomy.gov). The vehicle's MSRP must not exceed $55,000 for cars or $80,000 for trucks, SUVs, and vans. Your Modified Adjusted Gross Income must not exceed $150,000 (single), $225,000 (head of household), or $300,000 (joint filers) in either the current or prior tax year.

Used EV credit: A credit of up to $4,000 (or 30% of the sale price, whichever is less) is available for used EVs under $25,000 purchased from a licensed dealer. Income limits are lower: $75,000 single, $112,500 head of household, $150,000 joint. The vehicle must be at least 2 years old. You can only claim this credit once every three years.

State credits may stack with the federal credit. California offers up to $7,500 through the Clean Vehicle Rebate Project (subject to income limits). Colorado offers $5,000. Many other states offer $500–$3,500 in additional incentives. Check dsireusa.org for your state's current programs. Our calculator shows federal credit only — add your state credit separately.
