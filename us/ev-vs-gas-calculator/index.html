How Much Does an EV Save vs a Gas Car in the USA?

The national average price of regular gasoline in the US sits around $3.45 per gallon in 2026, while the average residential electricity rate is approximately $0.16 per kWh. For most American drivers covering the typical 12,000 miles per year, this price gap translates into real, measurable savings every single month.

To put it in concrete terms: a gas car averaging 28 mpg burns through roughly 429 gallons of fuel per year at a cost of around $1,480. An equivalent EV consuming electricity at 3.5 miles per kWh uses about 3,429 kWh annually — costing just $549 to charge at home. That is a fuel saving of over $930 per year from charging costs alone, before you factor in reduced servicing and maintenance.

The concept of MPGe — miles per gallon equivalent — helps standardize these comparisons. The EPA sets 33.7 kWh as the energy equivalent of one gallon of gasoline. A car rated at 100 MPGe is using the energy equivalent of 100 miles per gallon, which at current US fuel vs electricity pricing means roughly three times cheaper per mile to run than a 33 MPG gas car.

It is important to note that electricity prices vary enormously across the United States. Washington State drivers enjoy rates as low as $0.10 per kWh, making EV running costs exceptionally cheap. At the other extreme, Hawaii residents pay around $0.38 per kWh — reducing the economic advantage of EV ownership significantly. California, despite high electricity prices of around $0.29 per kWh, still offers EV savings due to the state's extremely expensive gasoline. Use this calculator with your actual state electricity rate for the most accurate result.

Beyond fuel costs, EVs carry substantially lower maintenance costs. They have no oil changes, no spark plugs, no timing belts, no transmission fluid, and far less brake wear due to regenerative braking. The US Department of Energy estimates EV owners save an average of $800 to $1,000 per year on maintenance compared to equivalent gas vehicles. Over five years, total EV running cost savings commonly reach $6,000 to $9,000 compared to a comparable gas vehicle.

Do not overlook the federal Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) tax credit either. Qualifying new EVs are eligible for up to $7,500 in federal tax credits, dramatically improving the total cost-of-ownership comparison. Many states add their own incentives on top — Colorado offers $5,000, California up to $7,500 through CVAP, and New York up to $2,000. When purchase incentives are included, the EV vs gas comparison becomes even more compelling in most scenarios. Use the calculator above to model your specific situation.
